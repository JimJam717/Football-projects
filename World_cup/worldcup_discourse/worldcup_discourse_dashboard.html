Section 1: Overview

Reddit World Cup Discourse Analysis

Comments were automatically flagged for abusive language, identity-targeted content, or racism/discrimination discussion, then checked against sentiment analysis to see if the flags mean anything real.

Total comments analyzed: 136,110 sentiment-scored records, 21,433 flagged records.

Section 2

H4: Do flags reflect real hostility?

Confirmed
Flagged comments are negative 86% of the time versus 55% for unflagged, a 31 point gap.
Confidence

This difference is extremely unlikely to be due to chance.

Takeaway: the flagging system is catching real hostility, not noise.

Section 3

H1: Does match outcome affect hostility?

Null result
Rates are close across win, loss, and draw, with no large difference.
Confidence

The difference is statistically detectable, but too small to matter much.

Takeaway: losing a match does not appear to meaningfully increase hostile discourse compared to winning or drawing; this is a real and useful finding, not a failed test.

Section 4

Descriptive patterns to explore

Abusive or hard language is the largest flag category. The scan found 17,564 abusive or hard-language records, compared with 6,881 negative-match-tone records, 2,520 identity-targeted-context records, 840 racism/discrimination-discussion records, and 4 severe-identity-slur records.
Methodology note

Data source: Reddit comments from 14 matches in the 2026 World Cup project. Sentiment was scored with an XLM-RoBERTa model. Flag categories covered abusive language, identity-targeted content, and racism/discrimination discussion.
